Meet the first one.

The one who gets up when the alarm goes off. The one who works 160 hours this month, showing up early when the company needs it, staying late so the team doesn't fall behind, covering work that was never in the job description, putting in all the effort that never shows up on a paycheck.

At the end of those 160 hours, one number lands: about 5,400 dollars before taxes. And that number was decided by someone else.

This is your actual self. Who you believe you are right now. Your reality. Where you stand today.

Now meet the second one.

The one who lies awake and pictures something else entirely. Who sees a life that looks nothing like the first one's. Who has dreams, and aspirations, and a clear picture of who they hope to be.

This is your ideal self. Who you want to become.

And then there's the third.

The one who carries everyone else. The one who feels the responsibilities, hears the expectations, and keeps a quiet running list of everything they think they should be by now.

This is your ought self. Who you feel you should become.

Here's the problem: these three have never met.

The first one goes to work.

The second one dreams.

The third one worries.

And the space between them, between who you are, who you want to be, and who you feel you should be, is where the tension lives. Psychologists call it the self-discrepancy gap. You just call it that feeling you can't shake.

The gap is the issue.

Not your effort.

Not your talent.
